## What the numbers say

Latency. The baseline has the first sentence out in about 0.3 s (p50); Haiku
5.5 adaptive-low needs 1.8 s, Haiku 4.5 1.6 s. On plain one-round turns Haiku
5.5 is at 0.75–0.95 s and Haiku 4.5 at 0.6 s, against 0.2 s. The gap is widest
on tool turns: about 2.1 s for Haiku 5.5 adaptive-low and 1.8 s for Haiku 4.5
against 0.33 s. Adaptive thinking fires on the turns that are not plain chat
(tool turns, arithmetic, requests nothing can serve), which is why its overall
median sits far above its one-round median.

Tool use. Haiku 5.5 adaptive-low called the Tool on every turn that needed one
in all three passes; Haiku 4.5 on all but one turn of the first pass. The
baseline skipped a lookup on 2 of 16 tool turns here (1 and 4 in the earlier
passes) and then either invents facts — about the mine, the stranger, the
trader — or deflects.
qwen3.8 missed 3 of 16 (7 in each earlier pass). One tool call in 106 had
arguments that violate the schema (thinking-off: a relation outside the enum).

Thinking disabled. The Haiku 5.5 thinking-off arm wrote prose in front of a
tool call on 15 turns, and that prose is spoken before the Tool has run. Under
the production prompt most of it is in character ("Moment, ich würfle."); some
is not ("Brak fragt nach einer Zusammenfassung der letzten Sitzung. Ich rufe
den Recap ab." in pass 2). It also missed two tool turns in every pass. This is
the arm the adapter does not default to.

Leaks. No candidate produced a pseudo tool call or an element leak, none wrote
markdown, and no stage direction reached the voice (qwen3.8 wrote two in its
raw output; neither was spoken). The baseline's twelve "tool
markup" findings are closing audio tags — `[/cheerfully]`, `[/pause]`, once
`[/final]` — which the TTS markup has no such thing as. Once one is in a
script's history the model keeps writing them: here on nine of the ten turns of
one script. They would be sent to TTS.

Address. Haiku 5.5 called the bard Mira "mein Lieber" on 4 (adaptive-low) and
3 (thinking-off) turns, the baseline on 2, qwen3.8 on 3; Haiku 4.5 never did in
any pass.

The must-not-call dice turns. Two turns have a player report a roll they made
themselves. They contain "gewürfelt", so the production dice gate arms, and a
reply that repeats the player's number is taken for an invented roll: the guard
(`roll_claim`) regenerates it with the dice Tool forced. It fired on both turns
for every candidate but the baseline. Where the forced call goes through, the
NPC "rolls" as well and the turn fails its tool check; once (Haiku 5.5
adaptive-low) the regenerated reply then contradicted the forced roll twice and
the turn ended in the canned fallback line — the one error in the table. That
is a false positive of the guard, not of the models, and it is most of the gap
between "tools OK" and "tool turns OK".

Cost. Haiku 5.5 is about 1.6× the baseline per turn as metered ($0.075 against
$0.045 per 100 turns); Haiku 4.5 is 12×. The Haiku 5.5 figure is an upper
bound: its stable system prompt is now read from the prompt cache at a tenth of
the input rate, and the meter prices cached tokens at the full rate.

## Caveats

- 34 turns per candidate per pass: the p95 is the second-slowest turn, and
  behaviour counts move between passes (see above). Use `GX_LLMBENCH_REPEAT`
  before reading a small difference as real.
- LLM time only. No STT, no TTS: a first sentence at 293 ms is not first audio.
- The knowledge Tools answer from memory in microseconds; real tool rounds add
  their own query time, equally for every candidate.
- The detectors are heuristics. The reply dumps (`<candidate>.replies.md`,
  written next to the JSON reports, not committed) are the evidence; German
  naturalness was not scored at all and needs a human read.

## What the numbers say

Latency. The baseline has the first sentence out in about 0.26 s (p50); the
Haiku arms need 0.75–1.8 s. On plain one-round turns Haiku 5.5 is at about
0.8 s in both thinking modes and Haiku 4.5 at 0.65 s, against 0.2 s. The gap
widens on tool turns: 2.2 s for Haiku 5.5 adaptive-low and 1.9 s for Haiku 4.5
against 0.35 s. Adaptive thinking fires on the turns that are not plain chat
(tool turns, arithmetic, requests nothing can serve), which is why its overall
p50 (1.8 s) sits far above its one-round p50 (0.8 s).

Tool use. Every Haiku 5.5 adaptive-low and Haiku 4.5 turn that needed a Tool
called it (100 % of tool turns); the baseline skipped the lookup on 4 of 16 and
answered three of those with invented facts about Gellan, the mine and the
stranger. qwen3.8 missed 7 of 16 tool turns. Every tool call of every candidate
had schema-valid arguments.

Thinking disabled. The Haiku 5.5 thinking-off arm wrote prose in front of a
tool call on 14 turns, and that prose is spoken. Under the production prompt
most of it is in character ("Moment, ich würfle."), some is not ("Brak fragt
nach einer Zusammenfassung der letzten Sitzung. Ich rufe den Recap ab."). It
also missed two tool turns. This is the arm the adapter does not default to.

Leaks. No candidate produced a pseudo tool call or an element leak, and none
wrote markdown. The baseline's eight "tool markup" findings are closing audio
tags — `[/cheerfully]`, `[/whispers]` — which the TTS markup has no such thing
as; once one appeared in a script's history the model kept writing them. They
would be sent to TTS. Haiku 4.5 ended one pre-tool sentence in English ("Let
me save this:"), which is spoken. qwen3.8 opened one reply with "(lacht)" and
answered one turn with a lone emoji, which leaves nothing to speak.

Address. Haiku 5.5 called the bard Mira "mein Lieber" on 4 (adaptive-low) and
2 (thinking-off) turns, the baseline and qwen did on 2 and 4; Haiku 4.5 never
did.

The must-not-call dice turns. Two turns have a player report a roll they made
themselves. They contain "gewürfelt", so the production dice gate arms, and a
reply that repeats the player's number is taken for an invented roll: the guard
regenerates it with the dice Tool forced. It fired on both turns for four of
the five candidates (`roll_claim×2`), and where the forced call went through
the NPC "rolled" as well and the turn fails its tool check — a false positive
of the guard, not of the model. On the baseline the forced call failed
provider-side instead (`stream_error×8`) and the turn fell back to prose.

Cost. Haiku 5.5 is about 1.5× the baseline per turn ($0.075 against $0.048 per
100 turns); Haiku 4.5 is 12×.

## Caveats

- One pass of 34 turns per candidate: the p95 is the second-slowest turn, and
  behaviour counts move between passes. A pass an hour earlier (before the
  table gained its current columns) gave first sentence p50 / p95 of 318 / 886
  (baseline), 1874 / 3428 (Haiku 5.5 adaptive-low), 823 / 3678 (thinking-off),
  1419 / 4304 (Haiku 4.5) and 570 / 1794 (qwen3.8); tool turns OK 94 / 100 /
  81 / 94 / 56 %; and Haiku 5.5 gender slips of 9 and 7. Use
  `GX_LLMBENCH_REPEAT` before reading a small difference as real.
- LLM time only. No STT, no TTS: a first sentence at 263 ms is not first audio.
- The knowledge Tools answer from memory in microseconds; real tool rounds add
  their own query time, equally for every candidate.
- The detectors are heuristics. The reply dumps
  (`<candidate>.replies.md`, written next to the JSON reports, not committed)
  are the evidence; German naturalness was not scored at all and needs a read.

# llmbench results — 2026-11-22

All five candidates, the full corpus (4 scripts, 34 turns per candidate, 170
turns per pass), four passes over the day, `GX_LLMBENCH_DELAY=400ms`, candidates
interleaved per turn. Run from a developer machine (home broadband, Germany)
against the live Groq and Anthropic APIs; Anthropic through an OAuth access
token. How to read the columns: [`../README.md`](../README.md#output).

The tables below are the **fourth pass** (about 12:00 CET), the only one run on
the code as this file's commit has it. Passes 1–3 ran between 11:00 and 11:20,
before a review changed three things:

- the stage-direction sentence of the spoken-output rule was reworded (it no
  longer forbids the third person, and with audio markup it now says that a
  bracket is never an action);
- the detectors gained the *unknown [tags]* column, and two fact-question
  turns now allow a knowledge lookup;
- a prompt-cache write is metered at 1.25x.

The behaviour figures quoted for passes 1–3 are **re-scored** from their stored
replies with the current detectors and corpus (`TestLLMBench_Rescore`); their
latency and cost are as measured. Passes 1 and 2 also predate prompt caching for
`claude-haiku-5-5`.

## What the numbers say

Latency. The baseline has the first sentence out in about 0.3 s (p50, 0.26–0.35
over the passes); Haiku 5.5 adaptive-low needs 1.8–1.9 s, Haiku 4.5 1.4–1.6 s.
On plain one-round turns Haiku 4.5 and Haiku 5.5 with thinking off are at
0.6–0.8 s and Haiku 5.5 adaptive-low at 0.95–1.4 s, against 0.2 s. The gap is
widest on tool turns: about 2 s for both Haiku models against 0.3–0.5 s.
Adaptive thinking fires on the turns that are not plain chat (tool turns,
arithmetic, requests nothing can serve), which is why its overall median sits
far above its one-round median.

Tool use. Haiku 5.5 adaptive-low never skipped a Tool a turn needed in any of
the four passes; its one failed tool turn (pass 4) made the right call and an
unneeded `transcript_search` beside it. Haiku 4.5 skipped one, in pass 1. The
baseline skipped a lookup on 3 of 16 tool turns in pass 4 (1, 4 and 2 in the
earlier passes) and then either invents facts — about the mine, the stranger,
the trader — or deflects. qwen3.8 missed 7 of 16 (7, 7 and 3 earlier) and left
one turn silent. All 108 tool calls of pass 4 had schema-valid arguments; over
the four passes one call did not (thinking-off, pass 3: a relation outside the
enum).

Thinking disabled. The Haiku 5.5 thinking-off arm wrote prose in front of a
tool call on 12 to 15 turns per pass, and that prose is spoken before the Tool
has run. Under the production prompt most of it is in character ("Moment, ich
würfle."); some is not ("Brak fragt nach einer Zusammenfassung der letzten
Sitzung. Ich rufe den Recap ab.", passes 2 and 4). It also missed two or
three tool turns in every pass. This is the arm the adapter does not default
to.

Stage directions and brackets. Asterisk- or parenthesis-wrapped actions and
third-person narration came from qwen3.8 only: one or two per pass in its raw
output, of which one was spoken in each of passes 1 and 2. That is not the same
as "no stage direction reached the voice" for the others: with asterisks
forbidden, models put the action into square brackets, where it looks like an
audio tag and is not one. The TTS prompt warns that a bracket outside its
vocabulary may be read aloud. What was spoken:

- Haiku 4.5, pass 2: six bracketed gestures — `[leans on the bar]`,
  `[leans back and thinks]`, `[nods approvingly]`, `[scratches head]`,
  `[leans on counter]`, `[beams and waves a hand]` — and `[lowers voice]`; one
  out-of-vocabulary tag in each other pass (`[singing, drawn out]`,
  `[shrugs in voice]`, `[chuckles nervously]`).
- The baseline, every pass: two to six of `[smiles]`, `[grins]`, `[smirks]`,
  `[schmunzelt]`, `[softly]`.
- Haiku 5.5: no gesture in any pass. Four invented delivery tags over passes
  1–2 (`[thoughtful]` three times, `[impressed]` once), none in passes 3–4, on
  either arm.
- qwen3.8: `[Lasses]` (pass 2), `[audio:long pause]` (pass 4).

Pass 4 ran with the reworded rule, which now says that a bracket is never an
action. No candidate bracketed a physical action in it (`[smiles]` twice from
the baseline is the nearest). One pass is not a measurement of the rewording:
Haiku 4.5's six gestures all fell in one of three passes under the old wording,
so a clean pass was already the usual outcome. Nothing strips an
out-of-vocabulary bracket on the way to TTS, and how the engine renders one was
not tested here.

Other leaks. No candidate produced a pseudo tool call or an element leak, and
none wrote markdown. The baseline writes closing audio tags — `[/cheerfully]`,
`[/pause]`, once `[/final]` — which the TTS markup has no such thing as: 19, 8
and 12 spoken in passes 1–3 (the *tool markup* column), none in pass 4. Once one
is in a script's history the model keeps writing them (nine of ten turns of one
script in pass 3). They would be sent to TTS. In pass 4 the baseline also spoke
a fragment of its own reasoning on one dice turn ("Oops, Sorry! … We need
correct response. Use proper tags."), which only the bracket check pointed at
(`[cheerliest?]`); the English detector needs three function words in a
sentence and missed it.

Address. Haiku 5.5 called the bard Mira "mein Lieber" on 4 to 9 (adaptive-low)
and 2 to 7 (thinking-off) turns per pass, the baseline on 1 or 2, qwen3.8 on 3
or 4; Haiku 4.5 never did.

The must-not-call dice turns. Two turns have a player report a roll they made
themselves. They contain "gewürfelt", so the production dice gate arms, and a
reply that repeats the player's number is taken for an invented roll: the guard
(`roll_claim`) regenerates it with the dice Tool forced. In pass 4 it fired on
both turns for every candidate but the baseline, where it fired on one. Where
the forced call goes through, the NPC "rolls" as well and the turn fails its
tool check; where the regenerated reply then contradicts the forced roll twice,
the turn ends in the canned fallback line (the baseline's one error in pass 4,
Haiku 5.5 adaptive-low's in pass 3). That is a false positive of the guard, not
of the models, and it accounts for two of the three failed turns behind Haiku
5.5 adaptive-low's "tools OK" figure.

Tool scoring. Until the review, a knowledge lookup on the two turns that ask
for a fact of the world (the menu and room price; the weather "for sure, not
guessed") counted as a tool error, although the Persona tells Flo to look
things up rather than guess. Haiku 5.5 adaptive-low did so in each of the first
three passes and was marked down for it; the baseline answered from imagination and was not. The
lookup is allowed now. "Tool turns OK" was never affected.

Cost. Haiku 5.5 is about 1.6× the baseline per turn as metered ($0.076 against
$0.047 per 100 turns); Haiku 4.5 is 12×. The Haiku 5.5 figure is an upper
bound: its stable system prompt is read from the prompt cache at a tenth of the
input rate, and the meter counts a cached read at the full rate (and a cache
write at the 1.25× it is billed).

## Caveats

- 34 turns per candidate per pass: the p95 is the second-slowest turn, and
  behaviour counts move between passes (see above). Use `GX_LLMBENCH_REPEAT`
  before reading a small difference as real.
- LLM time only. No STT, no TTS: a first sentence at 351 ms is not first audio.
- The knowledge Tools answer from memory in microseconds; real tool rounds add
  their own query time, equally for every candidate.
- The detectors are heuristics, and this file's first version shows what that
  costs: it reported "no stage direction reached the voice" from a detector
  that could not see a bracketed one. The reply dumps (`<candidate>.replies.md`,
  written next to the JSON reports, not committed) are the evidence; German
  naturalness was not scored at all and needs a human read.
